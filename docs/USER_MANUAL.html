About this manual

This manual is the authoritative operator and technical reference for callAPIcall, a suite of AI voice and telephony tools that run on a Windows 11 workstation. It is written for two audiences: operators who use the product day-to-day (Parts I and II), and integrators and developers who extend it or drive it through its programmatic interfaces (Part III and the appendices).

How to read it. Part I explains what the product is, how its pieces fit together, how to install it and how to configure every setting. Part II is a task-oriented user guide organised around the desktop Control Center and the everyday workflows (talking to the agent, placing calls, running the meeting copilot, cloning a voice, driving the avatar). Part III is an exhaustive reference to every software module, function and HTTP endpoint that makes up the system.

Conventions. Fixed-width text denotes code, file paths, configuration keys and literal values. Blue call-outs are notes, amber call-outs are warnings, green call-outs are confirmations and purple call-outs are tips. Function signatures are shown exactly as they appear in the source. Keyboard shortcuts are shown as Shift+F1. Network ports are given as :8770.

Scope & safety. Every default value, parameter name and endpoint path in this manual is taken directly from the shipping source code. The product places and answers real telephone calls, captures system audio and can synthesise a cloned voice and a talking-head avatar; operate it only where you are authorised to record and where all parties consent as required by local law.

Chapter 1

What callAPIcall Is

callAPIcall turns a Windows PC into an AI voice and telephony workstation: it talks with you by voice, conducts real cellular phone calls on your behalf, assists you live during video meetings, and can even appear as a talking-head avatar speaking in a cloned voice on a virtual webcam.

At the centre sits Hermes, a tool-using AI agent that runs natively on Windows. Around it, the call-bridge Python core wires together speech recognition, speech synthesis, telephony hardware, meeting capture and a desktop control application so that a single agent can be reached through many different "front doors" — a hotkey, a phone line, a meeting, or a phone in your pocket.

1.2 Who it is for

The product is a single-workstation system: everything runs on one Windows 11 machine (the audio hardware, the AI backends and the control surface), with only the optional Android companion app running elsewhere. It is designed for a technical individual or a small team who want a private, locally-hosted voice agent with real telephony and meeting integration, rather than a cloud service.

2.2 The audio-on-host constraint

Every audio path — Bluetooth HFP to the phone, microphone/speaker capture, STT and TTS — lives on the Windows host, because that is where the phone's Bluetooth Hands-Free endpoint is reachable. Handy and the native Hermes agent run on Windows too and talk to the core over localhost and stdio.

Historical noteHermes previously ran inside WSL2, which cannot see Bluetooth audio devices — hence the original "audio on the host" split. After the native cutover the constraint is historical, but the split architecture it produced is still the supported deployment model.
Chapter 3

Core Concepts

A handful of ideas recur throughout the product. Understanding them once makes every section of this manual easier to follow.

3.1 Half- vs full-duplex & barge-in

When you talk to the agent by voice there are two duplex modes:

  • Half-duplex (Shift+F1, speakers): while the agent is reasoning and speaking, your microphone is muted, so the speaker output cannot echo back into the microphone. Best when you are using loudspeakers.
  • Full-duplex (Shift+F2, headphones): the microphone is always live, so you can interrupt the agent mid-sentence. This interruption is called barge-in. An energy gate (RMS threshold, default barge_in_energy_threshold = 0.02) distinguishes real speech from residual TTS echo so that the agent is not interrupted by its own voice.

3.2 Call transports

Real phone calls can travel two ways, chosen by call_transport:

  • auto (default): detect and choose — prefer the USB GSM/4G modem, fall back to the Android phone over Bluetooth HFP.
  • gsm: force the USB modem (SIM800C on COM6 by default).
  • android: force the Android phone (HFP audio).
  • off: no automatic routing (legacy behaviour).

On the SIM800C/A7670E modems the audio is turn-based to files (record a window, synthesise a reply to a file, play it) because those chips do not expose a real-time USB audio stream. See Chapter 16.

3.3 The copilot

The meeting copilot (Shift+F4) captures the PC's loopback audio — everything the PC is playing, which is the other party's voice — and by construction never captures your microphone, so your own speech never triggers a suggestion. It runs STT on that audio, sends it to the agent, and shows a window with a one-line context summary plus copilot_num_responses (default 3) suggested replies. Optionally it can speak a chosen reply back (into the call via a virtual audio cable, on your speakers, or through the avatar). The window can be hidden from screen capture (copilot_hide_from_capture, default on).

3.4 The avatar

The avatar renders a chosen persona (a photo/clip in avatar/persone/<name>/) as a lip-synced talking head on the "OBS Virtual Camera" driver — no OBS Studio required. The first activation of a clip pays a one-time preparation cost (frame extraction, DWPose landmarks, VAE latents, blending masks) which is then cached to disk. At runtime an idle loop plays at 25 fps and each reply is pre-rendered (~1–3 s) and played back in sync with the audio.

3.5 Resilience

Two user-session supervisors (registered to autostart under HKCU) keep the call-bridge and the Control Center alive, restarting them within seconds of a crash. Stop sentinels (ptt.stop, cc.stop) mark deliberate stops so the supervisor does not fight an intentional shutdown. Restarting the core from the GUI is implemented as exit(42) followed by a supervisor relaunch.

Chapter 5

Configuration Reference

Every runtime setting lives in a single Config dataclass. This chapter documents how it is loaded and lists every key with its type, default and effect.

5.1 How configuration is loaded

Configuration is resolved in three layers of increasing priority by Config.load():

  1. Dataclass defaults — the values baked into callbridge/config.py (listed below).
  2. JSON file — config.json beside main.py (or an explicit path). Only keys present in the file are overridden.
  3. Environment variables — CALLBRIDGE_<KEY> in upper-case, highest priority. Booleans accept 1/true/yes; integers and floats are parsed by type; *_device keys accept an integer index.

The GUI writes changes back through Config.save(), which serialises the whole dataclass to config.json with two-space indentation. Helper accessors derive URLs: base_url() (phone), handy_base_url(), handy_stt_url() (ws://…/stt/stream) and handy_tts_url() (ws://…/tts/stream).

Change tokens before exposureThe shipped defaults auth_token, control_auth_token and gateway_auth_token are placeholders (change-me-*). Replace them before the gateway is reachable from another device.

Chapter 6

The Control Center

Overview

The Control Center is a desktop application (Windows) that manages an AI voice and telephony assistant. It runs quietly in the system tray and opens to a window with a left sidebar of sections and a main content area. The application talks to three local backend services:

  • Control server ("call-bridge") — the main backend, default http://127.0.0.1:8770, secured with a Bearer token read automatically from call-bridge/config.json. Drives sessions, calls, personas/avatar, meetings, analysis, config.
  • Handy — the STT/TTS engine, default http://127.0.0.1:8720. Drives voice cloning, TTS engine selection, model load/unload, audio previews.
  • wikibridge ("Knowledge") — the project knowledge/wiki server, default http://127.0.0.1:8775. Started on demand by the app the first time the Knowledge section needs it.

A colored dot at the top of the sidebar shows the control server connection: green "Connected" when reachable, red "Disconnected" when not. Live status is polled automatically — the voice session every 500 ms and the components/health every 5 s (with back-off when the server is down).

The sidebar sections are, in order: Session, Calls, Components, Guided Tests, Voices, Identities (Personas), Studio, Meetings, Knowledge, Analysis, Handy, Settings. A language selector sits at the bottom of the sidebar.

Section: Session

What it is for. This is the home screen. It starts and stops a live voice conversation with the assistant (the same thing the global hotkeys Shift+F1/F2 do), shows the current conversation phase, and controls the meeting Copilot feature. Use it for a hands-on spoken chat with the agent through your PC's microphone and speakers/headphones.

Controls / fields:

  • Phase pill — a colored badge showing the live session phase, polled every 500 ms: idle (grey), listening (blue), reasoning (amber, pulsing), speaking (violet, pulsing). Reads GET /session/state.
  • Mode label — when a session is active, shows the mode: *Full-duplex*, *Limited*, or *Half-duplex* (from session.mode).
  • Half-duplex card (speaker icon) — starts a session in half-duplex mode: your mic is muted while the agent speaks (best for open speakers). Calls POST /session/start with {mode:"half"}.
  • Full-duplex card (headphones icon) — starts a session in full-duplex mode with barge-in: you can interrupt the agent while it talks (use headphones to avoid echo). Calls POST /session/start with {mode:"full"}.
  • Close session button (red, appears when active) — ends the session via POST /session/stop.
  • A server-down banner appears if the control server is unreachable; the start cards are disabled. If a session is already running, attempting to start again produces a "session already active" message (HTTP 409).
  • Copilot panel: Status badge — active (green) / inactive (GET /copilot/state). Enable/Disable button — toggles the conversation copilot via POST /copilot/toggle (disabled if copilot_enabled is off). Project dropdown (book icon) — associates the copilot/session with a wikibridge project; sets POST /copilot/project. Save meeting button — analyzes the current copilot session and saves it as a meeting into the selected project's wiki via POST /copilot/save-meeting.

Procedure — hold a voice conversation:

  1. Confirm the sidebar dot is green (server reachable).
  2. Click Half-duplex (speakers) or Full-duplex (headphones).
  3. Speak; watch the phase pill cycle listening → reasoning → speaking.
  4. Click Close session when finished.

Procedure — capture a meeting with Copilot:

  1. (Optional) Pick a knowledge project in the dropdown.
  2. Click Enable on the Copilot panel.
  3. When the conversation is over, click Save meeting to analyze and file it into the project wiki.

Section: Calls

What it is for. Places and ends real phone calls in which the AI agent speaks with the person on the line, over whichever transport is available (a GSM modem or a connected Android phone via Bluetooth HFP). Also shows the live phone state.

Controls / fields:

  • Transport detected line — shows the currently selected transport (transport.selected) and its mode (transport.mode), from GET /transport.
  • Transport mode dropdown — *Auto* (""), *Force GSM* (gsm), *Force Android* (android). Passed as the transport field of the call.
  • Detect button (refresh) — re-probes transports via GET /transport?refresh=1.
  • Number field — the phone number to dial (required; trimmed).
  • Voice dropdown — the TTS voice for the call; populated from GET /models/available (tts.voices), each shown as "voice — language". Empty = default voice.
  • Language dropdown — *Italian* (it), *English (US)* (en-us), *French* (fr-fr), *Spanish* (es).
  • Call button (green) — dials. Internally it first calls POST /prepare (loads TTS, up to 4 min) then POST /call with {number, voice, lang, transport}. Disabled while busy, unreachable, or already in a call.
  • Hangup button (red) — ends the call via POST /hangup. Enabled only during a call.
  • Phone state indicator — colored dot + text, refreshed every 2 s from GET /status (phone.state): OFFHOOK (green, in call), RINGING (amber), otherwise grey/"Unknown". Shows the number when present.

Procedure — place a call:

  1. Click Detect to confirm a transport is available (or leave *Auto*).
  2. Type the destination Number.
  3. Optionally choose a Voice and Language.
  4. Click Call; the app prepares TTS then dials. Watch the phone-state dot.
  5. Click Hangup to end.

Section: Components

What it is for. A live health dashboard and start/stop/restart control panel for the backend building blocks: the main bridge, the remote gateway, Handy (STT/TTS), Hermes (the agent), and the avatar worker. Component health is polled every 5 s from GET /components/status.

State colors: running (green), loading/stopping (amber), blocked (orange), error/unreachable (red), stopped (grey), disabled (faint).

Cards and their controls:

  • Bridge (main) — the core control server. Shows PID when up. When reachable: Restart and Stop buttons (POST /components/main/restart|stop). Chapters 13–14

Entry Points & Core Orchestration

This chapter documents the command-line entry point and the core orchestration modules of the call-bridge subsystem: the CLI, the orchestration engine, the configuration layer, the HTTP control server, the "brain" factory, shared utilities, and the local Hermes stub. Every default, parameter name, and behaviour is quoted directly from the source.

Module: main.py — CLI entry point

main.py is the executable entry point for the call-bridge — the AI telephone-agent host on Windows. It parses a subcommand and dispatches to one of several run modes: audio-device enumeration, connectivity checks, outbound/inbound calls, the headless service, the push-to-talk user session, the voice gateway, or a single combined instance. It wires together the configuration, the orchestrator (CallBridge), the various brains, the control server, the voice gateway, and the push-to-talk/overlay stack. STT and TTS are real WebSocket clients to Handy; Hermes is real (HermesClient, HTTP/SSE) when hermes_mode == "http", otherwise a local echo stub. When ACP is enabled, the full agent is reached over ACP instead.

Command-line subcommands

Invoked as python main.py <cmd> [args]. A subcommand is required.

SubcommandArgsPurpose
devices—Lists the detected HFP (Bluetooth Hands-Free) audio devices.
ping—Verifies the connection to the Android control app.
callnumber (required)Calls a number and starts the conversation (e.g. +39333xxxxxxx).
answer—Waits for one incoming call and answers it (one-shot).
serve—Starts only the orchestrator control server.
server—Headless service: control + gateway + media (without PTT).
ptt—Starts only push-to-talk/overlay (user session).

--no-gateway (on all) skips the voice gateway in this process (it runs in the headless service instead). At startup main() calls _setup_logging(), then cfg = Config.load(), and dispatches on args.cmd.

Functions

_setup_logging() -> None

Configures the root logger at INFO with a timestamped format.

_cmd_devices() -> None

Resolves HFP input/output indices via find_hfp_devices() and prints the downlink (input, interlocutor's voice) and uplink (output, synthesised voice) devices.

_cmd_ping(cfg) -> None

Constructs a PhoneControlClient(cfg) and calls phone.health(). On success prints OK + phone.status(); on failure prints guidance and sys.exit(1).

_cmd_call(cfg, number) -> None

Creates CallBridge(cfg, StubHermes()), starts an outgoing call via start_outgoing_call(number), and joins the driver thread. Ctrl+C hangs up and joins with a 3 s timeout. This CLI path always uses StubHermes (local echo).

_cmd_answer(cfg) -> None

Creates CallBridge(cfg, StubHermes()), starts start_incoming_answer(), and joins. Same interrupt handling as _cmd_call.

_cmd_serve(cfg) -> None

Imports and calls run_server(cfg) (blocking) — the plain control-server mode.

_build_brains(cfg, log)

Creates the tuple (brain_full, brain_limited, brain_copilot) for push-to-talk. FULL (F1/F2) = make_full_brain(cfg, label="ptt"). LIMITED (F3) reuses the same instance as full when acp_enabled (a single hermes process, not two), otherwise make_limited_brain(cfg). COPILOT (Shift+F4) is None unless copilot_enabled; when enabled it is a full brain if copilot_use_acp, else limited — a dedicated instance so its context is not mixed with the PTT's.

_start_gateway_thread(cfg, log, critical=False) -> None

Starts the voice gateway in a daemon thread if gateway_enabled. When critical=True (headless service) a gateway death exits the process with code 42 so NSSM cleanly relaunches; when False (main.py all, PTT is primary) the failure is only logged.

_build_ptt(cfg, log)

Builds overlay + copilot + PTT manager without starting anything, returning (overlay, manager, copilot). Separated from the mainloop so _cmd_all can hand the manager/copilot to the control server (/session/*, /copilot/*) before blocking on the Tkinter loop. It also warms up the ACP agent in a background acp-warmup thread so the first F1 does not pay the ~65 s agent-startup cost. The overlay (tkinter) must run on the main thread.

_run_ptt_mainloop(cfg, log, prebuilt=None) -> None

Runs the rich PTT (overlay + F1/F2/F3) on the main thread (blocking). Calls manager.start(), prints the hotkey banner, runs overlay.mainloop(), and in finally always calls manager.stop() so global hotkeys are de-registered.

_cmd_ptt(cfg) -> None

Push-to-talk + overlay only (user session). In the split architecture the control server, gateway and media server run in the headless service; this keeps only what needs the interactive session. It does not start a gateway here (that would double-bind on gateway_port).

_cmd_all(cfg, no_gateway=False) -> None

Single instance: control server + voice gateway + PTT in one process. Builds PTT first (so GUI endpoints operate on the same hotkey instance), starts the control server in a control-server thread with its internal PTT disabled, starts a models-keepalive thread (reloads Handy models every 600 s, under the 900 s idle-unload), optionally starts the gateway, then runs the PTT mainloop (blocking). no_gateway=True skips the gateway (it runs in the headless service).

_cmd_server(cfg) -> None

Headless service (Session 0): control + gateway (critical=True) + media, without PTT/overlay. Starts the models keep-alive, then runs run_server(cfg, start_ptt=False) (blocking). GUI /session/* and /copilot/* do not operate here (the PTT manager lives in the user-session process); the hotkeys remain fully functional there.

main() -> None

Sets up logging, builds the argparse parser, parses args, loads config, and dispatches. For gateway it calls run_gateway(cfg) directly; for all it passes no_gateway=args.no_gateway.

Operator procedures

  • Enumerate audio devices: python main.py devices.
  • Test the Android app link: python main.py ping (exit 1 on failure).
  • Place a call (stub brain): python main.py call +39333xxxxxxx.
  • Answer one incoming call: python main.py answer.
  • Control server only: python main.py serve.
  • Headless service (NSSM): python main.py server (exits 42 if the gateway dies).
  • User-session PTT only (split): python main.py ptt.
  • Voice gateway only: python main.py gateway.
  • Everything in one process: python main.py all (add --no-gateway when the gateway runs in the service).

Module: orchestrator.py

The call-lifecycle orchestrator. CallSession represents an active phone call (a StreamingSession plus a watcher that closes the session when the call returns to IDLE). CallBridge manages the whole lifecycle — preparing models, placing/answering calls, running the streaming session — and is the entry point used by both the CLI and the control server.

class CallSession(StreamingSession)

__init__(self, config, hermes, in_idx, out_idx, hfp_rate, voice, lang, phone) -> None

A single phone call's session. config (Config); hermes (the brain, duck-typed respond(text) -> Iterator[str], or a HermesSession); in_idx/out_idx (HFP device indices); hfp_rate (negotiated rate); voice/lang (TTS); phone (a PhoneControlClient).

_extra_threads(self) -> list

Returns one daemon state-watcher thread targeting _state_watcher.

_state_watcher(self) -> None

Every poll_status_interval_s, if phone.status()["state"] == "IDLE" it logs, sets _stop, and returns (the far end hung up). PhoneError is swallowed and retried.

class CallBridge

__init__(self, config, hermes) -> None

The orchestrator: call lifecycle + streaming sessions. Creates a PhoneControlClient, a HandyControlClient, and a session lock. hermes is a HermesClient or a stub with .respond(text).

prepare(self, stt_model=None, tts=True) -> dict

Loads models into VRAM ahead of a call via handy.load_models(...); returns Handy's result.

start_outgoing_call(self, number, voice=None, lang=None, stt_model=None) -> threading.Thread

Starts an outgoing call (non-blocking); returns the call-driver thread. None voice/lang/stt_model fall back to the config defaults.

start_incoming_answer(self, ring_timeout=30.0) -> threading.Thread

Waits for one incoming call, handles it, then terminates (one-shot). Returns the answer-driver thread.

start_incoming_watcher(self) -> threading.Thread

Continuously watches for and handles every incoming call (the incoming-watcher thread).

hangup(self) -> dict

Stops the active session (if any), then calls phone.hangup(). Returns {"status":"ok"} or {"status":"error","message":...}.

status(self) -> dict

Returns {"session_active", "phone", "handy"} (phone/handy errors embedded as error fields).

Internal drivers. _drive_outgoing_call (load models → resolve devices → dial → wait OFFHOOK → run session); _handle_incoming_call (read caller, derive lang/voice via voice_lang_for_number, load TTS, resolve devices, answer, run session); _wait_for_incoming (poll until RINGING+INCOMING); _run_session (atomically reserve the session slot, wrap a HermesClient in a HermesSession with notify_*/call_ended, run the CallSession, safe-hangup on error); _resolve_devices (autodetect HFP indices + negotiate rate); _wait_for_state; _safe_hangup.

The shared bidirectional session used by both phone calls (CallSession) and push-to-talk (PushToTalkSession). Key concepts (for users)

  • Half- vs full-duplex. Half-duplex (F1/speakers, F3/limited) mutes the mic while the assistant thinks and speaks (plus a short post-TTS cooldown), stopping the mic from re-hearing the assistant. Full-duplex (F2/headphones) keeps the mic open, so you can barge in.

Chapter 16

Telephony & Audio Modules

This chapter covers the physical/transport layer that connects the assistant to real phone calls. There are two independent paths: Android + Bluetooth HFP (the phone dials on the cellular network and routes call audio to the PC as an audio device, controlled by an HTTP PhoneControlClient), and a direct GSM/4G USB modem (a SIM-equipped USB modem dials directly; on the SIM800C the audio is exchanged turn-based via files on the modem's flash).

Hardware concepts

Transport auto-selection

call_transport = auto prefers the USB GSM/4G modem and falls back to the Android phone over HFP. The two paths are complementary: the Android HFP path uses PhoneControlClient (HTTP to the phone app) for control and HfpAudioIO for real-time, full-duplex, low-latency audio; the direct GSM path uses GsmModem (AT commands) for control and file-based, turn-based audio (CREC/CMEDPLAY), one fixed-window turn at a time with no downlink VAD.

Why real-time USB audio is unsupported on the SIM800C

The SIM800C only exposes call audio on analog pins that are not broken out on this board — there is no USB audio interface. So voice moves to file, turn-based: play a file into the call with AT+CMEDPLAY, record the call to a file with AT+CREC, and transfer files over serial with the flash file system (FSCREATE/FSWRITE/FSREAD/FSFLSIZE/FSDEL). The A7670E 4G dongle on COM9 likewise has no usable USB audio stream and uses a different AT dialect. True real-time USB call audio would need a module that presents a USB sound card (e.g. Quectel EC25 / SIMCom SIM7600).

Flash file system

fs_size(filename) → AT+FSFLSIZE; fs_write(filename, data) → FSCREATE + FSWRITE (raw bytes, > prompt, ESC-abort on failure); fs_read(filename, chunk=4096) reads in blocks via AT+FSREAD=<file>,1,<want>,<offset> (reads exactly payload+terminator because the bytes "OK" occur naturally inside binary audio), rerouting call-drop URCs; fs_delete(filename) → AT+FSDEL.

In-call audio (turn-based)

play_media(filename, path=3, volume=80) → AT+CMEDPLAY=1,"<file>",<path>,<volume> (toward the caller); stop_media() → AT+CMEDPLAY=0; record_path(file_id=1) → C:\REC\<id>.DVI; record_start(file_id=1, channel=2) deletes any prior file then AT+CREC=1,<id>,<channel> (channel 2 = the caller's voice/downlink); record_stop() → AT+CREC=2.

Source note. The SIM800C's in-call audio routing (CMEDPLAY reaching the uplink, CREC capturing the downlink) is marked in the source as *not yet validated with a live SIM*; the file system is confirmed. Recordings are DVI/IMA ADPCM (via audioop) while TTS playback uses AMR-NB (via ffmpeg) — two different codecs on the two directions.

Module: gsm_call.py

Orchestrates a turn-based voice conversation over an already-established SIM800C call. Each turn: the AI speaks (text → Handy TTS → PCM → AMR-NB → FSWRITE → CMEDPLAY), then listens (CREC for a fixed window → FSREAD → DVI/ADPCM → PCM 16 kHz → Handy STT → text), then the brain replies. No live audio ⇒ no downlink VAD ⇒ a fixed window (gsm_record_window_s) per turn.

Answers (ATA), waits 1 s, runs the conversation with opening "Pronto?".

The main loop, guarded by call_deadline = now + gsm_max_call_s and empty_turns. It ends on the deadline, on _ended (call dropped), or after gsm_max_empty_turns consecutive silent turns. Per turn: speak any opening, _listen(gsm_record_window_s), then _think/_speak. finally always hangs up.

_think / _speak / _listen / _stt

_think(text) streams the brain and returns the reply. _speak(text) synthesises via Handy TTS, encodes to AMR-NB, fs_writes to gsm_tts_file, and play_medias it at gsm_play_volume (then sleeps the clip duration). _listen(window_s) records channel 2 for a fixed window, reads the .DVI, decodes via dvi_to_pcm, and runs _stt. _stt(pcm16k) streams to a HandySttClient in ~100 ms blocks and returns the joined text.

AI Service Clients

Module: acp_client.py

Connects to the full Hermes agent over ACP (Agent Client Protocol), a newline-delimited JSON-RPC 2.0 protocol over the agent process's stdio. Unlike HermesClient (limited phone-server over HTTP), this reaches the complete agent with all tools/actions. It launches hermes acp (WSL or native), maintains a persistent session (preserving context), and exposes respond(text) -> Iterator[str] — a drop-in brain. The validated flow is initialize → session/new → session/prompt, with reply text arriving as session/update notifications of type agent_message_chunk. Reverse requests are handled: session/request_permission is auto-allowed (autonomy); fs/* and terminal/* are declined.

Initialises process handle, inbound queue, request-id counter, session id, a prompt lock and a start lock, and a _last_fail timestamp for a 30-second start cooldown. No process launched here.

Starts the agent ahead of time (best-effort) so the ~90 s startup happens once, not on the first utterance.

True only if started and the process is alive. Used by /diag/acp.

_start_locked(self)

Launches the process and performs the handshake. WSL mode (acp_use_wsl true): wsl.exe -d <distro> -- bash -lc <acp_wsl_command>, cwd acp_cwd. Native mode (false): the command parsed from acp_native_command via shlex.split(posix=False), cwd acp_cwd_native, optional HERMES_HOME=acp_hermes_home, launched with CREATE_NO_WINDOW (no console pops up under the PTT supervisor). Sends initialize then session/new (reads sessionId), each within acp_init_timeout_s.

_ensure_started(self) -> bool

Returns True if alive. Applies a 30 s cooldown after a failed start (avoids repeated acp_init_timeout_s waits), then tries _start_locked() under the start lock.

close(self) / end(self)

close() clears state and terminates the process (3 s wait). end() is a no-op (the ACP session is persistent, keeping context).

_handle_reverse(self, msg) -> bool

session/request_permission → selects an "allow" option (auto-allow) or cancels; fs/*/terminal/* → JSON-RPC error -32601.

respond(self, text, preamble=None) -> Iterator[str]

The primary brain API. Acquires the prompt lock. If _ensure_started() fails, yields the speakable fallback "Scusa, l'assistente non è al momento disponibile." and returns. Drains stale messages, sends session/prompt with {sessionId, prompt:[{type:"text", text: (preamble or acp_voice_preamble) + text}]}, and yields agent_message_chunk text until the request id closes or acp_response_timeout_s. In finally it calls _cancel_turn(rid) on barge-in/timeout so aborted-turn chunks are not spoken next turn.

STT/TTS with Handy (:8720). Control (HTTP /models/*), STT stream (ws://…/stt/stream, 16 kHz PCM in, JSON utterance/speech_start out), TTS stream (ws://…/tts/stream, config/text/flush/end in, audio_start+binary+audio_end out).

Module: meetbot/bot.py — MeetBotSession

Part A — Android Companion App

The Android app ("callAPIcall Voice", Capacitor + React + TypeScript, Zustand, react-i18next, Tailwind) is a remote control for the PC-side voice assistant. End-user guide

Connecting to the gateway (over Tailscale). In Settings set the Gateway URL (e.g. ws://100.x.y.z:8771, the Tailscale IP of the PC running Handy + the gateway; shipped default ws://:8771) and the Token (must match the backend gateway_auth_token; default placeholder change-me-control-token). The app is configured for cleartext HTTP/WS (androidScheme:"http", cleartext:true) since it reaches the backend over Tailscale/LAN without TLS. The header Wi-Fi icon is green when connected (ready).

Choosing the audio output — this changes how you talk. The three output buttons at the bottom select where the voice plays and the interaction model: Speaker (viva voce) → turn-based (push-to-talk, because the speaker would echo into the mic); Earpiece and Bluetooth → full-duplex (talk and listen at once). Talking in Speaker mode (hold-to-talk + drag-up lock). The round Talk button is press-and-hold: hold and speak, release to send ("hold" turn). While holding, drag up ~60 px to *lock* continuous streaming (the backend ends the turn on a natural pause). The button colour reflects the phase: listening (accent), reasoning (amber), speaking (pulsing).

Talking in Earpiece/Bluetooth mode (tap to call). The button becomes a phone toggle: tap once (green) to open a continuous full-duplex session; tap again (red) to end.

Attaching an image. Tap the header image icon; the photo (JPEG, quality 80, base64) is attached to the *next* message — a toast confirms, then speak to send it. Receiving media. Generated images/videos appear as chat bubbles; long-press (≈550 ms) or right-click a media bubble to Save locally (to the device Documents folder, callapicall_<timestamp>.jpg/.mp4). Part B — Avatar / Lipsync Video Worker (avatar/avatar_worker.py)

A dedicated Python process implementing the video avatar on MuseTalk V1.5, running in musetalk-venv with CWD = the MuseTalk repo. The orchestrator launches it via avatar_client.py and speaks JSON lines on stdin; the worker emits JSON event lines on stdout. Video streams frame-by-frame to an OBS virtual camera; audio plays to a configurable sound device.

stdin commands: {"cmd":"persona","dir":...,"clip":...} (load a persona: pick a clip, run MuseTalk preparation — cached on disk, first time takes minutes — then idle); {"cmd":"speak","wav":...} (lipsync the audio and play it); {"cmd":"interrupt"} (barge-in, handled directly by the stdin reader so it works mid-utterance); {"cmd":"quit"}. stdout events: ready, persona_ready (clip, frames), speaking, progress, idle, interrupted, error. Constants: FPS = 25, EXTRA_MARGIN = 10, PARSING_MODE = "jaw", BATCH_SIZE = 12.

Startup gotchas. Monkeypatches torch.load to weights_only=False (trusted legacy MuseTalk checkpoints); inserts MuseTalk/ on sys.path and chdirs into it; imports DWPose/scipy eagerly before transformers (importing them lazily inside prepare_clip deadlocks the Windows DLL loader).

class PrepProgress

Emits throttled (≤2/s) progress events and keeps per-phase speed stats in cache/prepare-stats.json (EMA). Phases: extract, landmark, latents, masks (masks = 2×n for the forward+reverse loop). ETA blends the live speed of the current phase with historical stats for future phases.

class VideoSink

Tries pyvirtualcam.Camera(w, h, fps=FPS) (OBS virtual camera); falls back to an OpenCV preview window. send(frame_bgr) outputs (BGR→RGB for the cam).

class Engine — MuseTalk model host and pipeline

Loads models (unet.pth, sd-vae, musetalk.json) at half precision, plus Whisper and FaceParsing. prepare_clip(clip_path, cache_dir) is the prepare/cache pipeline: cache hit (reload) or extract frames → landmarks (chunks of 25) → VAE latents (crop+resize 256×256) → forward+reverse loop build → blending masks; persists coords.pkl, mask_boxes.pkl, latents.pt and removes the raw frames. lipsync_stream(mat, wav_path, start_idx) yields lipsynced frames one at a time in GPU batches of 12 (Whisper audio features + UNet + VAE decode + blend).

play_wav_async(wav_path) / main()

play_wav_async plays the wav to AVATAR_AUDIO_DEVICE (env; else default). main() is the idle/speak loop: builds the Engine, emits ready, and a reader() thread reads stdin (interrupt sets an event directly; other commands queue; quit breaks). Persona command: reads manifest.json, picks a clip at random, prepare_clip, (re)builds the sink at the clip resolution, emits persona_ready. Speak command: a producer thread runs lipsync_stream into a bounded queue, a prebuffer collects BATCH_SIZE frames (so audio+video start together), then play_wav_async starts and a consumer renders at 25 fps anchored to the audio clock (discarding late frames, holding the last on a gap). Interrupt stops audio and drains. Idle: cycles the prepared loop at 25 fps.

Persona manifest. A directory with manifest.json containing a "clips" list; the worker caches prepared material under cache/<persona>__<clip-stem>/. Env AVATAR_AUDIO_DEVICE sets the output; weights live under ./models/musetalkV15/ and ./models/whisper/.

Appendix C

Glossary

ACP
Agent Control Protocol — JSON-RPC over stdio used to talk to the native Hermes agent.
Barge-in
Interrupting the agent's spoken reply by talking over it (full-duplex only).
Brain
A configured agent instance bound to a hotkey or channel (full, limited, copilot).
HFP
Hands-Free Profile — the Bluetooth profile carrying call audio to/from the phone.
Half/Full duplex
Whether the microphone is muted while the agent speaks (half) or always live (full).
Handy
The Rust/Tauri STT+TTS engine on :8720.
Loopback
Capture of the PC's own output audio (WASAPI) — the other party's voice in a call.
MuseTalk
The GPU lip-sync model powering the avatar worker.
Persona
An avatar identity: a folder with a manifest (voice + video clip).
PTT
Push-to-talk — the hotkey-driven voice conversation subsystem.
Split architecture
The supported deployment: headless gateway service + interactive PTT process.
Transport
The physical path a phone call takes: USB GSM modem or Android over HFP.
Appendix D

Troubleshooting

Common symptoms and where to look. Most conditions are also surfaced by the Control Center's Components dashboard and Guided Tests.

SymptomLikely causeWhat to do
Sidebar dot is red / "Disconnected"The call-bridge control server (:8770) is down.Open Components → Bridge card → Start (clears the ptt.stop sentinel and launches the supervisor). Confirm with python main.py ping.
Pressing Shift+F1 does nothingPTT disabled, hotkey owned by another app, or the PTT process is not in the user session.Check push_to_talk_enabled=true; ensure the split PTT task is running (main.py all --no-gateway); a headless-service-only deployment has no hotkeys.
Session starts then immediately stops (start_failed)Handy models not loaded, or the audio device is unavailable.Open Handy → Load; verify the microphone with Guided Tests → /diag/mic; set ptt_input_device/ptt_output_device if the default device is wrong.
Agent's first reply takes ~60–90 sThe ACP agent is cold-starting (loading tools/MCP).Expected once per process; the PTT process warms the agent in the background at launch. Keep the process resident.
Agent speaks its tool calls aloudAn over-heavy acp_voice_preamble disrupts tool-calling.Keep the voice preamble minimal (the shipped default is minimal); the TTS sanitizer already strips markup.
Echo / the agent interrupts itselfSpeaker audio re-entering the mic in half-duplex, or the barge-in gate too low.Use headphones (F2) for full-duplex, or raise barge_in_energy_threshold; tune ptt_post_tts_pause_s / ptt_listen_warmup_s.
No transport for a phone callGSM modem not registered, or the Android control app unreachable.Calls → Detect; for GSM confirm SIM registration (python -m callbridge.gsm_modem status); for Android verify the phone app and auth_token.
GSM call audio is silentThe SIM800C's in-call file audio routing is not yet validated on the hardware.Known limitation (see Chapter 16); the flash file system works but in-call CMEDPLAY/CREC routing awaits SIM validation.
Handy build fails (whisper-rs-sys)LLVM 22 breaks bindgen.Install libclang==18.1.1 via pip and point LIBCLANG_PATH at it (see Chapter 4).
Avatar preparation seems stuckFirst-time clip preparation is genuinely slow (minutes), or the GPU hung.Watch the progress bar/ETA; there is no hard timeout, but a no-progress watchdog (avatar_prepare_stall_s, 300 s) aborts a true hang.
Virtual camera not found in Meet/ZoomThe "OBS Virtual Camera" DirectShow driver is not installed.Install the standalone driver from drivers/obs-virtualcam/ (no OBS Studio required).
Copilot window is blankLoopback capture unavailable, or nothing is playing on the PC.Guided Tests → loopback (/diag/loopback); ensure the other party's audio plays through the default output; install soundcard if prompted.
Meeting bot cannot joinPlaywright/Chromium missing, or the bot is stuck in the waiting room.pip install playwright; playwright install chromium; an organiser must admit the guest within 10 minutes; check the join-error.png/not-admitted.png screenshots in the meeting artifacts folder.
Calendar events do not appearOAuth not completed, or the token expired.Meetings → connect Google/Outlook via the OAuth wizard; Google apps left in "testing" expire every 7 days — publish the consent screen.
Gateway unreachable from the phone appWrong Tailscale IP/port or token mismatch.Set the app's Gateway URL to ws://<PC-tailscale-ip>:8771 and the Token to match gateway_auth_token.
First line of defenceThe five Guided Tests in the Control Center exercise the whole pipeline step by step and pinpoint the failing stage faster than any manual check.
